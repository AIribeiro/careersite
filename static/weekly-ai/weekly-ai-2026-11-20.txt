This is Weekly AI, covering November sixteenth through twentieth, twenty twenty-six.

Four developments this week point to one enterprise leadership question: can we demonstrate that AI agents are working within their authority, and delivering results worth the cost?

First, Microsoft announced an AI Transparency Initiative with more than forty organizations. The goal is to make agents' actions independently verifiable, even when businesses use different models and providers.

Next, SnapLogic published a survey of one thousand IT decision-makers. Fifty-four percent reported abandoning agentic AI projects because the business value was unclear. This is a vendor-sponsored, self-reported finding, not a verified failure rate.

In the United States, Reuters reported that two senators warned the White House about AI-enabled cyber threats outpacing defenses.

And on Friday, Reuters reported European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde's concern about AI-amplified cyber threats to finance.

For me, these stories belong in the same management discussion. Before expanding an agent's authority, I would ask who owns the workflow, what independent evidence records its actions, what outcome justifies its cost, and how we can intervene or recover.
