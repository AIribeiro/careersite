# Editorial format selection · Executive AI Briefings

Effective 31 October 2026. This is the shared channel-selection rule for the portfolio's Thinking articles, Executive AI Briefings, LinkedIn Article Studio / The AI Enthusiast News, LinkedIn Leadership Visibility, Daily Activities and selective social distribution. This document does **not** change the WEEKLY AI Friday schedule, add a publication quota or authorize publication.

## Editorial promise

Help busy enterprise leaders make better AI decisions. Put business ownership, measurable changes in work, cost/quality/speed, governance, adoption, evidence and actual trade-offs ahead of tools or hype. Use a distinctive, evidence-grounded first-person perspective only where warranted; no invented personal anecdotes, confidential cases, unsupported outcomes or exaggerated certainty.

## Choose the medium *after* choosing the reader's problem

| The reader needs… | Primary treatment | Selection test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| To make or challenge one specific enterprise AI choice | **Executive AI Briefing** | Can a decision-owner use it to ask better questions, identify the accountable owner and decide what to verify or do next in a few minutes? |
| A nuanced, argued perspective or substantial first-hand leadership analysis | **Thinking article** | Does a defensible thesis, counterargument, evidence and consequence require room to develop? |
| The week's verifiable external AI developments and their shared leadership implication | **WEEKLY AI** | Does the current Monday–Friday reporting window supply 3–5 independently verified relevant developments? Keep the existing Friday contract. |
| An original broader editorial discussion for the subscriber community | **The AI Enthusiast News** newsletter | Is there a developed argument and enough verified supporting material for a LinkedIn-native article? |
| One timely professional viewpoint or a useful discussion trigger | **LinkedIn native post** | Can the central argument stand independently as a short coherent post? Link contextually, not to fill a promotional quota. |
| One verifiable published line of relevant thought | **Daily Quote** | Is there a fresh verbatim eligible quote and does the existing approval workflow authorize it? Do not manufacture quotes or extra posts. |

A news event may *inspire* an evergreen Executive Briefing, but avoid treating a press summary or general primer as a decision guide. A valuable topic can produce a briefing first and, only if materially different, a later essay/newsletter. Never repackage the same copy across all channels.

## Executive Briefings — mandatory release standard

The authoritative writing, fact-checking, design and release checks for every new or substantially revised edition are in **[Executive AI Briefings — editorial and visual quality standard](executive_briefings_editorial_standard.md)** (effective 2 November 2026).

It is **not** a reusable layout or a license to borrow third-party infographic structures. The executive scan must be quick, but the complete edition may run 4–7 minutes or longer when complexity justifies it. Accuracy, original explanation and useful next steps take precedence over any rigid length target.

Every Executive AI Briefing must use field-informed, anonymous examples and must never identify Jair's current or former employers, even in disclaimers, footnotes, source links, images or metadata. Do not publish invented first-person cases or unverifiable business outcomes. This rule is defined in the authoritative quality standard and applies to existing editions when edited.

No new briefing should be called publication-ready until the six mandatory review criteria are addressed. Keep database publication, code deployment and live visual verification as distinct states; never assert unobserved checks. Do not publish merely to keep a cadence.

## Executive Briefing selection gate

Select or propose an Executive Briefing **when most of these conditions hold**:

1. **Specific senior decision**: a choice about AI investment, capability fit, process redesign, ownership, adoption, risk, scaling or measurable value—not an open-ended technical explainer.
2. **One clear judgment**: state what should be decided, challenged or ruled out; distinguish recommendation from conditional judgment.
3. **Useful in a leadership meeting**: preferably three to five sharp questions/checks and the evidence needed to answer them, not generic advice.
4. **A visible trade-off**: explain what could invalidate the recommendation, whose permission/decision rights matter and what might fail.
5. **An immediate next move**: an identifiable owner, decision test or action and what business outcome would show progress.
6. **Proof and originality**: any case is truthful and permissible to share; externally changing claims are dated and sourced; no recycled generic content.

If the subject needs depth, publish a Thinking article and, only if a genuinely distinct executive quick-reference emerges, propose a companion briefing. If there is no meaningful choice or practical next step, do **not** label it a briefing.

## Briefing writing and reader experience

- Lead with **the decision** and **the executive call**. A reader must understand the judgment and immediate next move in roughly **one minute**. Follow with enough verified evidence, exceptions, trade-offs and a concrete decision record to make the full edition credible: typically **4–7 minutes when justified**, shorter for a straightforward choice or longer when the subject demands it. Remove filler, not necessary substance.
- Make it self-contained and actionable: one focused question, an informed recommendation, a readable decision map/checklist/table only where it clarifies the choice, a realistic example with explicit evidence status, and a concise contact CTA for business-specific depth.
- Use restrained navy/copper portfolio visuals, readable enterprise-grade icons and graphics that actually explain the decision. Never substitute decorative art or technical diagrams for content. Check desktop/mobile hierarchy and content integrity before publishing.
- Every published briefing is an **independent Content Studio item** with `kind='Executive Briefing'`, visible as its own card on `?page=briefings` and opening in its own reader route. Preserve its title, metadata, references and page URL. Do not render all briefing bodies inline on the library page or link to a missing draft.
- No automatic generic scenario, unverified quantification, fabricated first-hand claim or thin three-question placeholder passed off as a completed executive guide.

## Editorial discovery, cross-linking and distribution

1. For *each* editorial selection or due LinkedIn post, consult the **currently published** portfolio catalog and the Executive AI Briefings collection before choosing a destination. Match the **actual question or decision** in the source content; do not favor a briefing merely because the section is new.
2. Prefer a verified **individual briefing page** for a post about that briefing's specific executive question. Use the verified **library index** only when the post introduces the collection or several related briefings. For broader analysis, prefer the most relevant verified Thinking article or another portfolio page.
3. LinkedIn native posts should independently teach something. Where the existing LinkedIn Visibility rule calls for one published portfolio link, this can be **one Executive AI Briefing instead of one Thinking essay**. Preserve the existing native post cadence and the no-extra-announcement rule; don't turn each briefing into a separate promotional post.
4. For LinkedIn newsletter contextual crosslinks, a verifiably **published and relevant individual briefing can count as one distinct portfolio article/page** where the existing three-link rule accepts a CMS-published page. Keep three distinct relevant live links and natural anchor text. Never force three briefings, repeat one link or cite the library index as three different publications.
5. Use verified **first-party original reader URLs** with appropriate real channel/campaign/role attribution on LinkedIn, as required by current policy. Do not reuse `utm_source=chatgpt.com` in a LinkedIn or other social post. Other channels retain their own existing verified short-link policies; never invent an alias or imply a link was checked when it wasn't.
6. In WEEKLY AI, cite original news publishers for news facts. A link to a relevant existing briefing can be *supplementary reading* only where it adds real value; never displace the sources or the prescribed Friday synthesis. No new standalone Weekly AI post or cadence.
7. When there is no relevant **verified published** briefing, choose the best existing article, or write the post without a forced link and disclose the exception. Do not promote planned/unpublished entries or pretend to have verified a blocked Streamlit page.

## Quick editorial decision record

For each proposed content item or promotion, internally record: **reader / question / recommendation / selected format / reason briefing fits or does not / exact published destination / cross-link role / duplicate check / next action**. Report only the useful outcome, not a scoring ritual.

Measure qualifying engagement using the existing first-party portfolio analytics and validated links where available: briefing opens, meaningful reads, downstream exploration and contact intent. Keep visitors and sessions distinct; do not call a click a conversation, a contact a client, or unobservable social impressions verified.

## Boundaries

This is a **selection and editorial-quality rule**, not a publishing instruction. CMS publication, social publishing, newsletter issuance, plugin changes, or automation schedule changes require their existing approvals and permissions. Retain the Friday WEEKLY AI contract, existing editorial ownership, Content Studio roles and the two-current-briefings independent-reader architecture.
