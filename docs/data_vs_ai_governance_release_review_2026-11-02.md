## Editorial intent and distinction from the library

The decision for a senior leader is not whether to create separate committees. It is how to require two credible evidence streams, define distinct responsibilities and authorise a single AI-enabled business workflow only when the decision is defensible.

Existing library items separately cover AI governance control layers, data-governance funding and AI Act role classification. This edition addresses the **boundary and handoff between data governance and AI governance**, including what each proves, where controls meet and who owns the release decision. It is not a six-row redraw of the source image.

**Executive call:** Do not merge distinct responsibilities or create competing approval empires. Require information-fitness evidence and AI operating-assurance evidence, then record one accountable business decision.

**Original diagram:** Data governance evidence stream + AI governance evidence stream → one release gate. The four internal questions concern information, permissible use, system behavior and operational intervention. These are an author's management lens, not a borrowed taxonomy or legal checklist.

## Field-informed, anonymized example

A service team reviews technical cases, knowledge articles and routing recommendations. Three contrasting failure modes are used:
- Current AI behavior, outdated or poorly governed source.
- Trustworthy source, incorrect AI application of an exception.
- Acceptable content, AI system has too much permission to act or disclose.

This synthesizes recurrent enterprise data-quality, service-process and AI-use decision situations. No employer, client, supplier, proprietary product, project detail, personal name or invented result is included. The body explicitly labels the example an **illustrative composite** rather than an employer case.

## Primary reference validation, as of 2 November 2026

1. **EDM Association, DCAM v3**: https://edmcouncil.org/frameworks/dcam/ — data-management capabilities, business data knowledge, operating model, evidence-based assessment. An industry framework, not binding law.
2. **NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core**: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/ — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage, lifecycle-based AI risk and accountable executive decisions. A voluntary framework; a future revision is being prepared.
3. **GDPR / EU Regulation 2016/679**: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng — legal scope for personal data, not a definition of the full data-governance discipline.
4. **EU AI Act consolidated 27 July 2026**: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng — amended regulation, referenced with dates/status caveat.
5. **AI Act Articles 10 and 26**: https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-10 and https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26 — data governance, input-data fitness, deployer oversight and monitoring where scope and relevant application dates are satisfied.

The briefing avoids the source infographic's jurisdiction-specific incident-notification comparison; no unqualified legal deadline is used as a general business rule.
