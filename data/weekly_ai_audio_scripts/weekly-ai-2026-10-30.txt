Welcome to Weekly A I, the Friday perspective from my A I and Data Portfolio. This week, the question isn't how many agents a company can launch. It's who is responsible when those agents begin doing real work.

Microsoft's latest customer examples offer one practical answer. It says Truist now oversees more than thirteen thousand A I agents, each with an accountable owner, and automatically disables agents that sit unused for forty-five days. That's a reported practice, not proof of business impact.

Reuters tells a related story: Serval has raised new funding to automate IT workflows, but its chief executive says customers still generally want human approval for sensitive changes.

At Goldman Sachs, David Solomon argues that businesses deploying A I must own the consequences, alongside regulators and model developers.

And in a World Economic Forum conversation, economist Daron Acemoglu reminds us that the direction of A I matters for workers and shared prosperity.

My takeaway from leading enterprise A I and data initiatives: define ownership before delegation, build controls into everyday processes, and judge progress by outcomes for people and customers.
